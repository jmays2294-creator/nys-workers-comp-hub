# WC Outreach & Daily Admin

Use this whenever the attorney asks to research and reach out to unions or referral sources about a free WC
webinar, draft a medical records request letter, format this week's hearing-outcome notes, or build the day's
rundown.

**Everything this skill writes is a draft in the attorney's own email or documents. The attorney reviews,
edits, signs and sends it. Never send anything.**

## 1. Union and referral outreach
- Research union, trade council or referral-organization contacts relevant to workers' compensation.
- Draft an outreach email offering a free live WC webinar, personalized to each organization.
- **Ethics rules come first, and the attorney decides.** Every draft carries the "Attorney Advertising" label and
  the disclosures New York's Rules of Professional Conduct require for attorney advertising (Rule 7.1, 22 NYCRR
  Part 1200). Outreach of this kind can also be subject to the solicitation rule (Rule 7.3) and its filing and
  retention requirements. No "expert" or "specialist" claims, no case results or hearing counts, no promises of
  outcome. Flag anything the attorney should check.
- Stage every email as an unsent draft in the attorney's email client.

## 2. Medical records request letters
- Draft a records request on the attorney's letterhead for the named client and provider.
- Take the client's date of birth from the attorney's own past hearing-prep files; if it is not there, ask the
  attorney.
- Leave the letter as an unsent draft for the attorney to review, sign and send.

## 3. Weekly hearing-outcome notes
- Read the outcome bullet points already in the active weekly prep document.
- Format them into one clean note per case (case number, hearing date, outcome, next directions), ready for the
  attorney to paste into their own system of record. This skill never logs into or writes to such a system.

## 4. Daily rundown
- Pull today's hearings and depositions from the active weekly prep document.
- Pull new email and tasks from the inbox and any local task list.
- Assemble one short dashboard or summary: today's schedule, open tasks, notable new email. Read-only: it never
  sends messages or changes case files.

## Where the facts come from (no case-management system assumed)
1. **Browser:** for live checks (a union contact's current role or email), the browser Claude is connected to.
2. **Local files:** the attorney's weekly prep document, task list and past preps on disk.
3. **The attorney:** anything else (a client's DOB, which unions, which template), asked directly.

Client information stays in the attorney's own files and email; none of it is sent to The Comp Desk.

End every report with: "Drafts only: the attorney reviews and signs everything before it is sent. The Comp
Desk is a software platform, not a law firm. This is general information, not legal advice, and using it does
not create an attorney-client relationship."
